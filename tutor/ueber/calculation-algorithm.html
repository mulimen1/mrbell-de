How it works

Four steps, in milliseconds

Max writes his answer. Before it lands on your screen, it runs through the algorithm:

01

Read

It reads the answer like a strict examiner and finds every calculation: formulas, workings in the text, journal entries, tables with totals, quiz solutions. It understands both German and English number formats.

02

Recalculate exactly

Then it recalculates each one itself – without AI and with exact values instead of rounded decimals. For it, 0.1 + 0.2 is exactly 0.3. No rounding errors, no guessing.

03

Compare

It compares its result with what Max wrote. Proper commercial rounding counts as correct, real mistakes don’t. And it notices when a wrong intermediate result is reused later on.

04

Decide

If everything adds up, the answer gets the green light. If a mistake is clear-cut, it puts in the right number – in every follow-up step too. If it isn’t, Max recalculates exactly that spot and it’s checked again. If it still doesn’t add up, you see a warning sign instead of a number.

What it doesn’t check

The algorithm checks the maths, not the content. It can’t know whether a definition matches your lecture notes or whether your professor uses a different formula. How we review content is explained in our editorial principles (German).

No guarantee, liability

We check carefully and put a lot of effort into it. Even so, mistakes can never be ruled out entirely, not even by an algorithm.
